VALUING PLAYERS FOR THIS SURVIVOR LEAGUE

Recommendation: use external projections for the first version, build the league
valuation layer ourselves, and measure the benefit of custom projections later.
The history is valuable because it describes this specific auction room.

THREE OUTPUTS THAT SHOULD REMAIN DISTINCT

1. Fair statistical value: the contribution to an eight-category roster, converted
   to auction dollars using the league's available budget and roster constraints.
2. Expected auction price: what this league is likely to pay, learned from genuine
   opening auction purchases and comparable player profiles.
3. Recommended bid ceiling: a decision for a particular team, accounting for its
   keepers, category balance, open slots, remaining dollars and survival risk.

Keeper surplus is fair market value minus the actual next-season keeper charge.
Do not assume that next year's keeper charge equals the latest recorded salary.
Keeper escalation, eligibility and maximum tenure still need confirmation.

EIGHT-CATEGORY CONTRIBUTIONS

Assumed categories: PTS, REB, AST, STL, BLK, 3PM, FG%, FT%; no turnovers.
First convert each player's projections to the appropriate time horizon and
expected usable starts. A full-season games estimate is not automatically right
for a survivor league, particularly when cuts begin early or statistics reset.

For a first baseline, standardize contributions within the relevant rosterable
player pool. Compare against replacement at the actual league depth and positions.
Rare blocks or steals can become valuable through their standardized contribution
and replacement scarcity. Do not arbitrarily double them and then count that same
scarcity again. Both percentages and stocks can also have empirical premiums in
your league's auction prices; estimate those separately from their roto value.

Use shooting volume, not raw percentages:
  FG impact = FGM - (pool FG% * FGA)
  FT impact = FTM - (pool FT% * FTA)
Pool percentages must be weighted by attempts. Standardize these impacts alongside
the six counting categories. A low-volume accurate shooter can have less impact
than a slightly less accurate player taking many shots. Large negative shooting
contributions must count too. Do not substitute eFG% or TS% for the actual categories.

Once a team roster is known, compute its exact combined makes/attempts and category
changes. The linear impact formula is a useful baseline, not an exact universal
dollar value for every roster. If historical category standings become available,
estimate how much change buys a standings point and test that against the baseline.

All-around stars should retain credit across all eight categories. Do not force
every player into one archetype. Multi-category strength and positional flexibility
can matter when assembling a roster that can survive, beyond a single raw stat.

HISTORICAL COMPARABLES AND PRICE MODEL

Join historical auction_sales to what was known before each auction: archived
preseason projections where available, otherwise lagged prior-season production,
age, role/minutes changes and known availability. Store the source and as-of date.
Current-year projections should be matched to comparable eight-category profiles,
including volume-weighted percentages, projected minutes and availability.

Use same-season realized actuals to evaluate outcomes and projection accuracy.
Do not train or backtest an auction-price forecast with end-of-season information
that bidders could not have known at the auction. A retrospective chart of what
production bought is useful descriptive analysis, but not proof of predictive power.

Show several comparable player-seasons with their actual auction price, statistical
profile and differences from the target player. Normalize category environments
within each year, account for league budget changes and keeper inventory, and give
recent seasons more relevance. Handle shortened seasons and unusual schedules
explicitly rather than comparing their raw totals to ordinary 82-game seasons.

Start with a simple regularized price model plus a weighted-comps estimate. Fit
nonlinear price behavior carefully: the top tier and the $1 floor differ. Benchmark
against a plain category-score-to-dollar curve before adding complexity. With
2,173 genuine auction observations, overly elaborate models can easily overfit.
Players with no close comparables, especially exceptional stars and rookies, need
wider uncertainty and explicit flags rather than forced misleading comparisons.

KEEPER INFLATION AND BUDGET RECONCILIATION

Remove next season's actual keepers from the auction pool. Subtract their charges
from league dollars and remove their occupied slots. Reserve the minimum legal
bid for every remaining slot before distributing discretionary dollars across
positive value above replacement. Check that implied auction allocations fit the
available money and roster slots. Record undrafted/zero-value outcomes explicitly.

An approximate inflation factor can compare remaining discretionary dollars to
remaining base value, but it should not assume keeper bargains always create the
same inflation for every player type. Scarcity depends on which categories and
positions have been kept. Do not count salary discounts as current auction sales.

SURVIVOR RULES CHANGE THE OBJECTIVE

Need: elimination dates and cadence, whether category statistics reset, active
lineup/bench/IL slots, games or start limits, and how eliminated rosters are acquired.
Those rules determine whether early usable production, stability, category balance,
or later schedule strength should carry the most value. A player returning from
injury in January may have appealing full-season stats and still be a poor purchase
for a team at risk of an earlier elimination.

Final rosters are useful for studying how teams evolved, but are selected by survival
and acquisitions. A champion's finishing roster is not an unbiased measure of its
auction quality. Do not use final finish as a causal label for an individual bid.
Eventually simulate league rounds and acquisitions after the rules and dated data
are available; do not claim a survivor-specific model before that is implemented.

VALIDATION AND DELIVERABLE

Hold out whole future seasons in chronological order. Tune only on older seasons;
never randomly split repeated player-season records across training and test data.
Compare dollar error, errors by price tier, ranking quality, interval coverage and
budget calibration. Evaluate actual fantasy contribution separately from predicted
auction price. Report outlier and unmatched-player coverage alongside average error.

The eventual draft board should show player, eight category contributions, usable
games/starts, fair value, expected auction price, bid ceiling, uncertainty range,
keeper eligibility/cost, closest historical comps, source date and risk notes.
