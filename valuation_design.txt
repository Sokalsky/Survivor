SURVIVOR VALUATION v1

THREE DISTINCT OUTPUTS

1. Survivor value score: season-equivalent eight-category advantage over a
   replacement pool that improves as teams are eliminated. Higher is better.
2. Expected league price: estimated winning bid from actual historical auctions,
   conditional on a player being drafted. A kept player's estimate is hypothetical.
3. Neutral auction value: an allocation of $3,000 across 225 slots in a fresh
   15-team, $200-per-team draft. No keepers, category punts or manager preferences.

Neutral dollars are model estimates of contribution, not an objective optimum or
personalized bid ceiling. Expected prices reflect this auction room's behavior;
they need not match neutral dollars and do not sum to a simultaneous auction.
The database's existing fair_value column stores neutral auction value for every
player, including keepers. Survivor score and decomposition live in category JSON.
recommended_bid_ceiling remains NULL; that is not one of the three outputs.

INPUTS AND RULES

500 complete, published CBS Sports 2026-27 forecasts supply games, minutes and all
eight categories. Original projections are unchanged. 6,460 historical NBA rows
cover 2014-15 through 2025-26. The league archive contains 2,173 actual purchases.
Keeper salaries and final-roster recorded costs are not price training examples.
The confirmed 30 keepers cost $790, leaving $2,210 for 195 available roster slots.

User-supplied survivor rules: 1,000 man-games, 100 roster moves. First elimination
after a month and a bit, then every one or two weeks until two teams remain.
Eliminated players enter regular waivers; remaining players enter a sniper draft
in reverse original order. A team cannot repeat a waiver position in a 15-year
cycle; year 16 resets it. Actual orders and dated acquisition logs are unavailable.

Roster size 15 is observed in the opening archive. A $1 minimum bid is an explicit
working assumption, consistent with the lowest recorded purchase. Exact active,
bench, IL and position rules are unknown. Categories are standard eight-cat roto:
PTS, REB, AST, STL, BLK, 3PM, FG%, FT%; no turnovers.

FORMULA

Select the 225 highest-minutes-per-game players as the fixed reference pool.
Pool FG% = sum FGM / sum FGA. Pool FT% = sum FTM / sum FTA.
FG impact = FGM_pg - pool_FG% * FGA_pg.
FT impact = FTM_pg - pool_FT% * FTA_pg.
Standardize each impact and the six counting rates by its pool mean and population
standard deviation. Q_i = sum of all eight z scores, equally weighted. Attempt
volume and harmful shooting count. Rare stocks earn large standardized effects;
there is no arbitrary extra stocks multiplier or star bonus. These are linear
shooting impacts, not exact roster-specific changes in percentages or standings.

For each stage t with T teams remaining:
  rosterable depth = T * 15;
  R_t = per-game score of the next player below that depth;
  fraction_t = stage weeks / season weeks;
  projected team games = sum published games of that top pool / T;
  usage_t = min(1, 1000 / projected team games);
  useful games_it = published GP_i * fraction_t * usage_t, if Q_i > R_t;
  survivor value_i = sum_t max(0, Q_i - R_t) * useful games_it / 82.

All category scores remain on the original reference scale. A top-30 per-game
player can contribute throughout the scenario; a fringe player earns value only
while above the rising replacement level. The uniform game-pacing factor keeps
each stage's neutral rosters within its share of 1,000 games. This is not a daily
lineup optimizer. Games are spread uniformly: it cannot distinguish an October
absence from a February absence. Delayed injury returns require particular care.

Neutral expected turnover after a cut from T teams is 15/T acquisitions per
surviving team. Replacement depth stops improving if those expected acquisitions
would exhaust the move allowance. Default scenarios use about 27.27 moves. This
neutral approximation does not assign waiver winners, recreate sniper picks or
prove every team gets that access. Additional streaming and injury moves are not
modeled. Unknown waiver positions are never randomly assigned.

Central scenario: first cut week 5.5, subsequent cuts every 1.5 weeks. Faster:
week 5 then weekly. Slower: week 6 then every 1.5 weeks. Thirteen cuts leave two
teams. A two-week interval throughout would exceed this season's available time.
Season length uses the NBA's October 20, 2026-April 11, 2027 calendar (174 days).
These are illustrative schedules, not confirmed league dates. The calculation
assumes cumulative stats and reaching the final; it does not forecast survival
probability or value winning an early elimination round separately. Exact dates,
statistics resets, position constraints and dated injuries remain unresolved.

Sort players by survivor score. Reserve $1 for each of the top 225, then allocate
the other $2,775 linearly by positive value above the first undrafted score.
Largest-remainder cent rounding makes the total exactly $3,000. Other players
receive $0. No price-fitted exponent changes neutral values. Keeper surplus is
neutral value minus the confirmed current keeper charge. It is not a prediction
of future keeper escalation, which is not yet specified.

MARKET PRICE MODEL AND COMPARABLES

For auction year Y, join prices only to regular-season statistics from Y-1.
Use a per-game reference pool with at least 10 games. There are 1,991 matches;
182 unmatched or low-sample purchases are exported with reasons. This excludes
rookies from validation, so their present-day price estimates are extrapolations.
Using Y's realized statistics to predict Y's preseason auction would leak future
information; those statistics never enter that auction's training features.

Features: eight per-game category z scores plus positive total score squared / 8.
Normalize historical paid dollars above $1 by that season's discretionary league
budget per open slot, after keeper costs. This assumes the current $200 budget
throughout history; incomplete roster entries and historical unspent dollars can
affect calibration. Feature centers/scales are fitted on training rows only.
Ridge regularization = 20; annual recency weight = 0.9. No age, role changes or
dated injury news are invented. Current features use published CBS projections.

Compare mean-price, simple total-score curve, ridge category model, 20 nearest
profiles, and a 50/50 ridge/comps blend. Select by MAE during 2018-19 to 2022-23,
then lock that choice for expanding-window tests in 2023-24 through 2025-26.
Every test year uses only earlier auction prices; earlier held-out years can be
included when fitting a later year, but do not reselect the model. Five displayed
comps use RMS distance across all eight z scores and show genuine original bids.
They explain statistical resemblance, not an exact average producing the price.

Selected model: ridge. Last three years: 541 matched purchases, MAE $4.86 versus
$5.33 for a simple score curve and $10.04 for the mean-price baseline. Purchases
costing $30+ have MAE $13.12 and mean prediction error -$11.19: stars are materially
underpriced. Do not hide this behind the overall error. The nominal 80% price
band achieved 78.2% chronological coverage. Bands use normalized absolute past
prediction errors by predicted price tier, falling back to all errors below 30
samples. Current price estimates and bands use the $2,210/195 auction context.

This is a price backtest using prior-year actuals, NOT a backtest of CBS forecasts,
this year's rookies or the survivor value formula. Archived historical preseason
projections and dated league standings/transactions would improve those tests.
Scenario dollar ranges are distinct from empirical market-price error bands.

DESCRIPTIVE ROSTER CHECK

Among the two finalists in each of ten usable seasons, 27/40 opening players
ranked top 30 by prior-year per-game stats appear on the same team's final roster;
32/98 ranked 31-100 and 6/145 ranked below 100 do. Players lacking prior samples:
3/17. Exclude 2016-17 because finish order is inconsistent. This supports examining
replacement risk but is selected on reaching the final. Matching snapshots cannot
prove continuous ownership, identify drop dates or estimate survival probabilities.
These results do not fit the neutral formula or historical price model.

OPERATION AND AUDIT

config/valuation.json records every setting; config/league.json records rules and
unknowns. Dashboard startup imports bundled stats, computes and transactionally
saves a versioned run. The run hash covers inputs, rules, settings and outputs.
Identical inputs do not duplicate runs; older runs remain browseable. Public API
requests remain read-only. No direct Railway access is part of development.

Local preview/export (memory only): python -m survivor.valuation --preview
output/valuations/survivor-valuations.xlsx contains all three outputs, category
contributions, actual comps, chronological tests, exclusions, scenarios and method.
CSV files and model-report.json allow independent review. They are exports, not
runtime imports. Railway computes the same run from its stored inputs on startup.
The website displays the saved model, assumptions, category bars, comps and price
bands and provides a valuation CSV download with availability filtering.
