SURVIVOR VALUATION v7

THREE DISTINCT OUTPUTS

1. Survivor value score: season-equivalent eight-category advantage over a
   replacement pool that improves as teams are eliminated. Higher is better.
2. Expected league price: estimated winning bid from actual historical auctions,
   conditional on a player being drafted. A kept player's estimate is hypothetical.
3. Neutral auction value: an allocation of $3,000 across 225 slots in a fresh
   15-team, $200-per-team draft. No keepers, category punts or manager preferences.

Neutral dollars are model estimates of contribution, not an objective optimum or
personalized bid ceiling. Expected prices reflect this auction room's behavior;
they need not match neutral dollars and do not sum to a simultaneous auction.
The database's existing fair_value column stores neutral auction value for every
player, including keepers. Survivor score and decomposition live in category JSON.
recommended_bid_ceiling remains NULL; that is not one of the three outputs.

INPUTS AND RULES

348 complete, published ESPN 2026-27 forecasts supply games, minutes and all
eight categories. Original projections are unchanged. 6,460 historical NBA rows
cover 2014-15 through 2025-26. The league archive contains 2,173 actual purchases.
Keeper salaries and final-roster recorded costs are not price training examples.
The confirmed 30 keepers cost $790, leaving $2,210 for 195 available roster slots.

User-supplied survivor rules: 1,000 man-games, 100 roster moves. First elimination
after a month and a bit, then every one or two weeks until two teams remain.
Eliminated players enter regular waivers; remaining players enter a sniper draft
in reverse original order. A team cannot repeat a waiver position in a 15-year
cycle; year 16 resets it. Actual orders and dated acquisition logs are unavailable.

Roster size 15 is observed in the opening archive. A $1 minimum bid is an explicit
working assumption, consistent with the lowest recorded purchase. Exact active,
bench, IL and position rules are unknown. Categories are standard eight-cat roto:
PTS, REB, AST, STL, BLK, 3PM, FG%, FT%; no turnovers.

FORMULA

Select the 225 highest-minutes-per-game players as the fixed reference pool.
Pool FG% = sum FGM / sum FGA. Pool FT% = sum FTM / sum FTA.
FG impact = FGM_pg - pool_FG% * FGA_pg.
FT impact = FTM_pg - pool_FT% * FTA_pg.
Standardize each impact and the six counting rates by its pool mean and population
standard deviation. Q_i = sum of all eight z scores, equally weighted. Attempt
volume and harmful shooting count. Rare stocks earn large standardized effects;
there is no arbitrary extra stocks multiplier or star bonus. These are linear
shooting impacts, not exact roster-specific changes in percentages or standings.

For each stage t with T teams remaining:
  rosterable depth = T * 15;
  R_t = per-game score of the next player below that depth;
  fraction_t = stage weeks / season weeks;
  projected team games = sum published games of that top pool / T;
  usage_t = min(1, 1000 / projected team games);
  useful games_it = published GP_i * fraction_t * usage_t, if Q_i > R_t;
  survivor value_i = sum_t max(0, Q_i - R_t) * useful games_it / 82.

All category scores remain on the original reference scale. A top-30 per-game
player can contribute throughout the scenario; a fringe player earns value only
while above the rising replacement level. The uniform game-pacing factor keeps
each stage's neutral rosters within its share of 1,000 games. This is not a daily
lineup optimizer. Games are spread uniformly: it cannot distinguish an October
absence from a February absence. Delayed injury returns require particular care.

Neutral expected turnover after a cut from T teams is 15/T acquisitions per
surviving team. Replacement depth stops improving if those expected acquisitions
would exhaust the move allowance. Default scenarios use about 27.27 moves. This
neutral approximation does not assign waiver winners, recreate sniper picks or
prove every team gets that access. Additional streaming and injury moves are not
modeled. Unknown waiver positions are never randomly assigned.

Central scenario: first cut week 5.5, subsequent cuts every 1.5 weeks. Faster:
week 5 then weekly. Slower: week 6 then every 1.5 weeks. Thirteen cuts leave two
teams. A two-week interval throughout would exceed this season's available time.
Season length uses the NBA's October 20, 2026-April 11, 2027 calendar (174 days).
These are illustrative schedules, not confirmed league dates. The calculation
assumes cumulative stats and reaching the final; it does not forecast survival
probability or value winning an early elimination round separately. Exact dates,
statistics resets, position constraints and dated injuries remain unresolved.

Sort players by survivor score. Reserve $1 for each of the top 225, then allocate
the other $2,775 linearly by positive value above the first undrafted score.
Largest-remainder cent rounding makes the total exactly $3,000. Other players
receive $0. No price-fitted exponent changes neutral values. Keeper surplus is
neutral value minus the confirmed current keeper charge. It is not a prediction
of future keeper escalation, which is not yet specified.

The reusable Razzball preseason URL is excluded because its contents mix seasons.
Five invalid publisher rows remain in rejected_records, never fitted or corrected

Only 2015-16 and 2016-17 still use preceding-season actual rates as proxies,
requiring at least 10 games. Never use the auction season's realized stats.
All forecast and actual profiles for a historical year use the same prior-year
reference distribution: 225 highest-minutes eligible players, fixed category
means, shooting baselines and standard deviations. Partial forecast coverage
does not define a new reference pool. Missing forecast GP is NULL and does not
participate in similarity; known GP differences remain a similarity dimension.
These dated sources are preseason evidence, not verified league auction-day
snapshots. Early publications may miss subsequent trades or availability news.
Partial top-player lists reduce coverage of cheap auction purchases, introducing
selection limitations that retrospective price errors cannot eliminate.

config/preseason_availability.json documents 16 season-specific availability
exceptions. They are excluded before both regression fitting and comp selection.
This includes Tatum 2025-26 and Klay 2019-20. The original purchase records and
keeper budgets remain untouched. Each exclusion includes a dated NBA source,
status and reason. Dates are checked against preseason, not unavailable league
auction dates. The list is incomplete; unlisted does not mean verified healthy.
It covers major rehabilitation, delayed starts and Wall's planned nonparticipation;
no actual games played later in the season determine an exclusion. No replacement
healthy price, zero projection, future return date or medical probability is made up.

Current fit: 1,589 purchases, including 1,203 forecast-based observations. Of the
2,173 total purchases, 34 lack a prior sample, 16 have documented availability
exceptions, and 534 have no usable forecast in their season's partial archive.
Exclusions are exported with reasons and source links.

Features: eight category z scores, positive score squared / 8; age-aware models
also use (season age - 27) / 5 and its interaction with positive score / 8.
Recorded ages come from prior-season Basketball Reference data only. 1,540 fitted
observations have age context. 332/348 current players have prior ages; the other
16 use the statistics-only counterpart. No ages or youth premium are assigned.
Ridge penalty is 20; recency weight is 0.9 per elapsed year. Model feature scaling
is fitted only on training observations. Current rates and GP are published ESPN
projections, not blended with the user workbook or altered to force player prices.

Each auction's budget is 15 * $200 minus that year's keeper costs. Reserve $1
per open roster slot; normalize bids above $1 by discretionary dollars per slot.
The available-talent candidate also multiplies this scale by:
  supply_ratio = (full-pool strength / 225) / (available strength / open slots)
Strength is positive per-game advantage above each pool's replacement. Historical
supply still uses prior-year actual players minus known keepers; the partial
forecast file cannot establish the full 225-player pool. Rookies, offseason role
changes, retirements and unavailable players limit this proxy. Today's supply
uses the complete ESPN set. Team budget dispersion is recorded, not simulated.

Selected model: market_age_local. Selection uses development auctions from
2018-19 through 2022-23 (610 purchases, MAE $4.6733), then stays fixed for the
later three seasons. Each fitted model uses only earlier auctions. No target
player's desired price is an input to comp selection. The v6 thresholds below
are explicit working rules. v7 retains them and changes the final comp influence.

COMPARABLE QUALIFICATION

Compare raw per-game stat lines on the target season's reference distribution.
For historical tests that distribution comes from the preceding actual season;
for today's players it is the current complete projection pool. Both sides use
the same shooting baselines and category standard deviations. This avoids calling
two players similar just because they have similar z scores in different eras.
Regression pricing retains each historical season's original relative strengths.

For each category, gap = (target contribution - comp contribution) / target SD.
Shooting contributions use makes above the common baseline, including volume.
Category RMS = sqrt(sum(gap squared) / 8).
Production gap = abs(sum(category gaps)); largest gap = max(abs(category gap)).
Age and games add distance penalties; they never dilute the eight-stat distance.
  distance = sqrt(RMS^2 + (production gap/4)^2 + (age gap/10)^2 + (GP gap/30)^2)

A close forecast match must pass every known-field limit:
  category RMS <= 0.75; production gap <= 3; largest category gap <= 1.25;
  age difference <= 5 years; projected games difference <= 15.
A supporting match must pass:
  category RMS <= 1.15; production gap <= 4; largest category gap <= 2;
  age difference <= 10 years; projected games difference <= 30.

Unknown GP/age stays unknown, contributes no distance term and is not a failed
limit. A close match therefore does not claim complete availability information.
Prior-actual proxies can only be supporting evidence. Documented preseason
exceptions remain excluded before fitting and comparison. No hard position rule:
category shape determines statistical similarity, without invented eligibility.

Keep up to 20 qualifying records, choosing close forecasts first, then by
smallest distance. Do not fill the list with disqualified players. Start with:
  raw weight = 0.9^(years since auction - 1) / (distance + 0.25)^3
  initial share = raw weight / sum(raw weights)
If close forecasts exist, give that group at least 80% of total weight, preserving
relative weights within each group. Thus one to three close forecasts dominate
supporting evidence; twenty weak records cannot dilute them. More than three
qualified forecasts are allowed. No supporting records means 100% goes to close
forecasts. Recency cannot promote a supporting record into the close group.

  residual = normalized actual price - regression price for the comp profile
  correction share = 0.75 if any close forecasts, otherwise 0.25
  adjustment = correction share * sum(weight * residual) * current market scale
  final price = clamp(regression price + adjustment, $1, $200)
No qualifying match means zero comp adjustment and the regression estimate alone.
The offset keeps exact matches finite; retained weights sum to 100%. Effective
comp count is 1 / sum(weight squared), or zero with no matches. Correlated seasons
for the same player are not independent evidence. GP is a similarity input, not
a fitted injury-price curve or dated return model.

In the drawer, the top three by weight sit beside the target's real projections,
including attempt volume and unknown GP. Adjusted price = budget/supply-adjusted
bid + (target regression price - comp regression price). It is a comp-implied price
before weighting and shrinkage, not the comp's actual bid or a new forecast.
Price effect = correction share * weight * (adjusted price - target base).
Individual price effects sum to the displayed comp correction. All qualifying
comps retain original bids, source basis, stats, match gaps and weights in the API
and workbook. Sorting changes display order only.

FINAL ESTIMATE INFLUENCE

The final price is also expressible as a convex blend, before the $1-$200 clamp:
  comp estimate = sum(comp weight * comp-implied price)
  final estimate = (1 - correction share) * base + correction share * comp estimate
Comp share and influence on the final estimate are different quantities. A comp
with 80% of the basket and a 75% basket blend has 60% final influence. v6 used
50% for the basket, giving that same comp only 40% influence. Supporting-only
players still use 25% of the comp estimate; no qualifying comps means 100% base.
The dashboard now displays both shares and a cash/supply/profile price bridge.

Five influence choices were specified before comparing historical errors:
  close group minimum 80%, final basket share 50% (v6), 75%, or 100%;
  close group minimum 90%, final basket share 75% or 100%.
Selection uses MAE on 610 development purchases, 2018-19 through 2022-23; each
fit uses only earlier seasons. The winner is 80% / 75%, MAE $4.6733 versus v6's
$4.6811. This development difference is small and does not establish statistical
significance. Later years do not select the influence setting. Their 100% blend
has lower MAE, but was not selected because it did worse on development years.
The frozen candidate set, all split metrics, and selected setting are saved in
output/valuations/comp-influence-check.json. Reproduce with:
  python scripts/check_comp_influence.py

0.4 BLK and 2.9 3PM, with unknown GP. He remains the one close forecast, receives
80% of comp weight and now has 60% final influence. Miller stays supporting.

13 then versus 17 now; the historical figure uses preceding-season actuals,
whereas the current figure uses projections. This is an imperfect supply proxy,

open slot: +$1.37. Account for available-pool strength: -$0.62, net $44.75.
The fitted stat/age profile difference adds $1.83, giving a comp-implied $46.58.

DESCRIPTIVE ROSTER CHECK

Among the two finalists in each of ten usable seasons, 27/40 opening players
ranked top 30 by prior-year per-game stats appear on the same team's final roster;
32/98 ranked 31-100 and 6/145 ranked below 100 do. Players lacking prior samples:
3/17. Exclude 2016-17 because finish order is inconsistent. This supports examining
replacement risk but is selected on reaching the final. Matching snapshots cannot
prove continuous ownership, identify drop dates or estimate survival probabilities.
These results do not fit the neutral formula or historical price model.

OPERATION AND AUDIT

config/valuation.json records every setting; config/league.json records rules and
unknowns. Dashboard startup imports bundled stats, computes and transactionally
saves a versioned run. The run hash covers inputs, rules, settings and outputs.
Identical inputs do not duplicate runs; older runs remain browseable. Public API
requests remain read-only. No direct Railway access is part of development.

Local preview/export (memory only): python -m survivor.valuation --preview
Age context can be rebuilt offline from verified HTML caches using:
  python -m survivor.market_context
The normal stats_workbook collector also refreshes it after complete historical
collection. Docker includes the age file; Railway reads it without fetching URLs.
output/valuations/survivor-valuations.xlsx contains all three outputs, category
contributions, actual comps, chronological tests, exclusions, scenarios and method.
CSV files and model-report.json include model comparisons and allow independent
review. Valuation exports are not
runtime imports. Railway computes the same run from its stored inputs on startup.
The website keeps the numeric outputs, category bars, comps and price bands.
Explanatory panels were removed at the user's request. Formula and source audits
remain here and in the model report. Board and CSV filters agree for search,
position, availability, metric and direction; missing numbers always sort last.
All nine partial archives are selectable on the projections page. Their JSON
files are imported idempotently into historical_projection_sets on normal Railway
startup. Current bundled IDs select model sources; stored prior versions remain
immutable. Published source dates and workbook received dates remain distinct.
